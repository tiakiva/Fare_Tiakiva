Plants and Trees of Polynesia

Discover the botanical richness of the Fenua 🌺

Fare Tiakiva is proud to present the project by 9th-grade students from Arue Middle School: “VEVO” or the echo of trees through the voices of children.

This educational project highlights scientific, cultural, and artistic research on some of the plants of Polynesia. The children share their knowledge with you and, above all, carry forward the connections between the land, plants, and people.

In Polynesia, when a child is born, we bury the placenta “Pu Fenua” and plant a fruit tree nourished by the placenta as the child was in the womb. The Polynesian is thus connected to the land and will always remember where they came from. The tree will bear fruit that will feed or heal. The land is already associated with the term “Pu Fenua,” as if the woman already carried the land within her.

QR codes in the garden will allow you to explore their work on various species present on the property. The children's voices will accompany you along the path of the plants like an echo from the trees themselves—to know them, respect them, and preserve them.

Auteraa (Casuarina equisetifolia)

A tree known for its medicinal properties, Auteraa is also valued for its resistance to coastal conditions. Its leaves are used in decoctions to relieve fever and pain, and it plays an ecological role in stabilizing shoreline soils.

→ Learn more about Auteraa

Auti (Cordyline fruticosa)

A sacred and protective plant, Auti is used in traditional ceremonies and as an ornamental plant. Its leaves are also used in traditional medicine and daily life for wrapping food or making leaf skirts.

→ Learn more about Auti

Coconut Tree (Cocos nucifera)

The coconut tree is the tree of life in the Pacific. It provides water, food, materials for construction, and even craft products.

→ Learn more about the Coconut Tree

Kahaia (Guettarda speciosa)

A very common coastal shrub, Kahaia is a small tree species from the Rubiaceae family.

→ Learn more about Kahaia

Mikimiki (Coprosma spp.)

Mikimiki is an indigenous shrub of Polynesia, known for its shiny small leaves and decorative berries. It is often used in ecological restoration and supports local biodiversity.

→ Learn more about Mikimiki

Tamanu (Calophyllum inophyllum)

Tamanu is a sacred tree for Polynesians. Its oil, extracted from the nuts, is highly valued for its healing and medicinal properties.

→ Learn more about Tamanu

Tiare Tahiti (Gardenia taitensis)

An emblem of French Polynesia, this highly fragrant white flower is often used to make welcome necklaces (hei) and in local cosmetic products.

→ Learn more about Tiare Tahiti

Tohonu (Cordia subcordata)

Also known as kanawa or tou, Tohonu is a small coastal tree valued for its sturdy wood used in traditional carpentry. It bears bright orange flowers and helps stabilize the coastline.

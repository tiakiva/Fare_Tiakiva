Autera’a

🌳 Nutritive and Medicinal Tree

Scientific name: Terminalia catappa

Family: Combretaceae

Tahitian name: Autera’a

Origin: Oceania (New Guinea, Polynesia, Malaysia)

Botanical Description

  • Type: Deciduous tropical tree
  • Height: Up to 25 meters
  • Leaves: Large, oval, arranged in spiral
  • Fruits: Elongated nut with an edible almond
  • Feature: Very characteristic horizontal branches

Traditional Uses

  • Food: almond of the fruit eaten by children
  • Traditional medicine: leaves and bark used for hypertension, liver disorders, asthma, diarrhea
  • Shade and ornament: planted in villages and along the coast
  • Local gastronomy: Tahitian tomme cheese flavored with autera’a nuts

📜 Explanatory Text

Autera’a Tropical Almond – Terminalia catappa Its scientific name is "Terminalia catappa" from the Combretaceae family. Its common names (country almond, pyé-zanmann) refer to the shape of the fruit resembling that of the European almond tree, but its botanical name is tropical almond. In French Polynesia, its Tahitian name is “autera’a”. Origin: Oceania (New Guinea, Polynesia, Malaysia). Habitat: All tropical zones of the globe. The natural spread of the tropical almond is due to the floating ability of its fruits across ocean currents: indeed, its nuts can float for a long time while preserving their ability to germinate. It was introduced to the West Indies in the 19th century (for shade and ornament). The autera’a can reach 25 m in height, and is very recognizable by the horizontal habit of its branches. The fruit contains a single, very hard seed with an edible almond inside, a delicacy that children in Polynesia love. They crack the shell with a stone to extract the nut. Uses: The almond tree is mainly used for shade, but also popularly for its numerous medicinal virtues (hypertension, liver disorders, asthma, diarrhea, aphrodisiac properties). The cheese dairy of Tahiti offers a tomme flavored with autera’a nuts.

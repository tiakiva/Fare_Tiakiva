Auti

🌿 Sacred and Ornamental Plant

Scientific Name: Cordyline fruticosa

Family: Asparagaceae

Origin: Southeast Asia, widely spread in Polynesia

Botanical Description

  • Type: Perennial tropical plant
  • Height: 1 to 3 meters
  • Leaves: Long, ribbon-like, green, purple or variegated
  • Flowers: Small, pink or purple
  • Fruits: Small red berries

Traditional Uses

  • Religious rituals: used in sacred ceremonies, symbolizes protection
  • Ornament: decorative plant in gardens and temples
  • Traditional medicine: leaf decoctions for fever and pain
  • Practical use: leaves used to wrap food, make skirts or roofs

📜 Explanatory Text

The leaves of the "ti" shrub (Cordyline fruticosa), among the most sacred species, were worn by orators, warriors, and priests when performing their duties. On the "Marae", they adorned priests, orators, and warriors during ceremonies. According to tradition, the god Ta’aroa created the "ti", now called "auti". A sacred plant, it was used to ward off evil forces. The "ti" is believed to originate from the bones of the hip and tibia, according to Polynesian beliefs. Originating from Indo-Malaysia, this plant was domesticated and widely spread across the Pacific, taking many forms. In Polynesia, it had several uses: its medicinal virtues were part of several "ra’au" (remedies) and its branches were used to set broken bones. Its very sweet root, cooked in large ovens for two to five days, provided nourishment during times of famine or the austral winter. At the end of the 18th century, it was the first plant to be distilled to make alcohol.

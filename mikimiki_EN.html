🌿 Miki Miki – Resilient Coastal Shrub

Miki Miki (Pemphis acidula) is a native shrub ranging from tropical East Africa to Eastern Polynesia. In French Polynesia, it is present across all archipelagos except the Marquesas Islands. Miki Miki is exclusively found in coastal environments, on sandy or limestone rocks, and more rarely on basaltic substrates. It is the most salt-tolerant species and often forms the first vegetation band on the ocean-facing side of motu or atolls. Its seeds disperse via ocean currents due to their high buoyancy. Miki Miki blooms and bears fruit year-round. It is a twisted shrub, growing up to 5 meters tall, with gray or blackish cracked bark. Its dark red wood is denser than water. Its leaves are simple, opposite, and narrowly elliptical, measuring 1 to 2.5 cm long. The flowers have six pleated white petals measuring 4 to 8 mm. The fruit is a brown capsule, 5 to 10 mm long, containing 15 to 20 elongated seeds, 3 mm long with thickened edges.

🔧 Traditional Uses

Miki Miki was highly valued in the past, especially in the Tuamotu archipelago where it is still abundant. It was used to craft objects requiring very hard and durable wood, such as spears, clubs, and fishhooks. To ensure durability, the wood was soaked in the sea before use.

🌺 The Legend of Heitarauri

The legend of Heitarauri tells the story of a young man from Bora Bora, raised by a pair of sharks, who traveled to the atoll of Marutea in the Tuamotu. Upon arrival, he resisted the queen's attempt to seduce him. As a memory of his journey—made standing on the back of a shark—he brought back a Miki Miki branch to Bora Bora.

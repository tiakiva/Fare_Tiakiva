Touhonu

By Kaha'i, Kialoa and Moemoeanaia

🌴 Guardian of the Polynesian Shores

Scientific name: Tournefortia argentea

Family: Boraginaceae

Origin: Coastal zones of Polynesia and other Pacific islands

Botanical Description

  • Type: Coastal shrub or small tree
  • Height: 2 to 5 meters
  • Leaves: Silvery, thick, fuzzy
  • Flowers: Small, white to violet, clustered
  • Blooming: All year round

Chemical Components

  • Volatile essential oils
  • Saponins
  • Tannins (antimicrobial properties)
  • Fatty acids and sterols

Traditional and Scientific Uses

  • Ecological: dune stabilization and coastal protection
  • Tradition: used for roofing or making floats
  • Traditional medicine: leaves applied to wounds, healing properties
  • Modern studies: antibacterial and anti-inflammatory activity

🌙 Touhonu in the Polynesian Soul

Touhonu Sentinel of the sands, tree of the wind, You watch over our silent shores, Shelter for turtle eggs and children, Noble tree with precious foliage. Your silvery leaves shine in the sun, Like a mirror offered to the ocean. Touhonu, you sing with the wind The living memory of the elders.
